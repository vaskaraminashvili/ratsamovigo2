## 2. Price range (comparable item: annual paid tier of a shared list app)
Lowest about $8.99 (Bring!), highest $14.99 (AnyList household), median about $9.99. At roughly 2.7 GEL per USD (approximate, check the rate) that is about 24 to 40 GEL per year. No Georgian competitor price was found.

## 3. Positioning map (price vs. Georgia fit)
- Low price, low Georgia fit: Bring!, OurGroceries, Listonic (global, English UI)
- Mid price, low Georgia fit: AnyList (meal planning, $14.99 household)
- Free, high Georgia fit, different job: Dazume, tarifebi.ge, Tetri (find cheap prices)
- Free, no fit to the job: family chats, Keep
- **Open spot:** a Georgian-first shared list with dishes and a weekly plan.

## 5. The gap (hypothesis)
None of the apps I found is a Georgian-language shared list that links dishes to ingredients and a weekly plan. The price apps (Dazume, tarifebi) solve "where is it cheap", not "what do we need and who buys it". The gap is plausible but unproven. The real competitor is the family chat, and there is no evidence yet that families will leave it.

## 6. The threat
- Dazume could add dishes and sharing fastest, since it already has Georgian store prices and a list.
- A global app (Bring!, AnyList) could add Georgian localisation cheaply.
- Your defence: Georgian product names, dishes suited to Georgian cooking, and per-store prices later (idea #5).
